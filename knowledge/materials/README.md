## 1. Overview
Traditional Indian water structures (Baolis, Kunds, Bawaris, Vavs) relied on localized, climate-resilient material systems:
- **Historic Sandstone (`historic_sandstone`):** Porous sedimentary ashlar blocks (Dholpur, Jodhpur, Dhrangadhra) requiring breathable lime joint networks.
- **Delhi Quartzite (`historic_quartzite`):** Dense crystalline metamorphic rock requiring flexible, thick-joint lime mortar to cushion stress and allow drainage.
- **Lakhori Brick (`lakhori_brick`):** Thin flat terracotta brickwork where joint mortar volume equals or exceeds unit brick volume.
- **Hydraulic Lime Mortar (`hydraulic_lime_mortar`):** Traditional kankar-derived hydraulic lime bedding.
- **Lime-Surkhi Mortar (`surkhi_lime_mortar`):** High-performance pozzolanic mortar blended with calcined clay and herbal admixtures (gur, urad, bael).
- **Fat Lime Putty (`fat_lime_putty`):** Slow-carbonating Class C lime for delicate non-submerged finishes.
- **Natural Hydraulic Lime (`natural_hydraulic_lime_nhl`):** Modern standardized conservation mortar (NHL 2 / 3.5).
- **Ordinary Portland Cement (`portland_cement_opc`):** Documented incompatible modern repair material.
- **Generic Acrylic / Epoxy Filler (`generic_acrylic_filler`):** Documented non-breathable, non-reversible synthetic filler.

## 2. Technical Boundary & Mandatory Verification
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Photographs alone CANNOT definitively determine the chemical composition of historic masonry or mortar.**
> 
> Visual observation provides hypotheses consistent with traditional material families. All physical restoration interventions require laboratory characterization (petrography, XRD, binder-aggregate ratio, soluble salt analysis) before execution.
